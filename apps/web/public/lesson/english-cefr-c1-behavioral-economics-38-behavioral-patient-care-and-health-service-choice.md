# C1 Vocabulary 38 — Behavioral patient care and health-service choice

This lesson follows how patients and caregivers interpret symptoms, appointments, treatments, costs, uncertainty, and trust across a health-care journey.

Flow: **diagnostic uncertainty perception → appointment effort cost → treatment side-effect salience → medical default → provider bedside trust → symptom escalation threshold → preventive reminder fatigue → health literacy friction → patient autonomy belief → waiting-list fairness → second-opinion motive → placebo expectation → adherence reward framing → care coordination trust → hospital reputation cue → medical bill shock → treatment uncertainty tolerance → caregiver decision burden → health insurance option overload → patient safety norm**.

## 1. diagnostic uncertainty perception /ˌdaɪəɡˈnɑstɪk ˌʌnsərˈtənti pərˈsɛpʃən/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** a judgment about how unclear the cause, seriousness, or likely course of a health problem is.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** judgment cause, seriousness hoặc course health problem unclear thế nào.
**Grammar & collocations:** `high diagnostic uncertainty perception` — perception; `calibrate diagnostic uncertainty perception` — calibrate.
**Examples:** `Diagnostic uncertainty perception made the patient postpone a decision until another test became available.` → Perception uncertainty diagnostic khiến patient postpone decision tới khi test khác available.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `진단 불확실성 인식`.

## 2. appointment effort cost /əˈpɔɪntmənt ˈɛfərt kɔst/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** time, travel, paperwork, and coordination required to arrange and attend a medical appointment.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** time, travel, paperwork và coordination để arrange và attend appointment medical.
**Grammar & collocations:** `high appointment effort cost` — cost; `reduce appointment effort costs` — reduce.
**Examples:** `Appointment effort cost was high for the caregiver who needed two buses and a day off work.` → Cost effort appointment cao với caregiver cần hai bus và nghỉ work một ngày.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `진료 예약 노력 비용`.

## 3. treatment side-effect salience /ˈtriːtmənt saɪd ɪˈfɛkt ˈseɪliəns/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** how noticeable and influential possible side effects are when a person evaluates a treatment.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** mức side effect possible noticeable và influential khi evaluate treatment.
**Grammar & collocations:** `high treatment side-effect salience` — salience; `reduce treatment side-effect salience` — reduce.
**Examples:** `Treatment side-effect salience made the patient focus on rare risks and overlook the expected benefit.` → Salience side-effect treatment khiến patient focus risk rare và overlook benefit expected.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `치료 부작용 현저성`.

## 4. medical default /ˈmɛdɪkəl dɪˈfɔlt/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** a preselected test, treatment, appointment path, or consent setting that remains unless actively changed.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** test, treatment, path appointment hoặc consent setting preselect giữ nếu không change.
**Grammar & collocations:** `safe medical default` — default; `review a medical default` — review.
**Examples:** `A medical default scheduled a follow-up call before the patient left the clinic.` → Default medical schedule follow-up call trước patient leave clinic.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `의료 기본값`.

## 5. provider bedside trust /prəˈvaɪdər ˈbɛdˌsaɪd trʌst/

**Core meaning (English):** confidence that a health professional listens, explains clearly, and treats a patient with care and respect.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** confidence health professional listen, explain clear và treat patient care, respect.
**Grammar & collocations:** `high provider bedside trust` — trust; `build provider bedside trust` — build.

**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `의료진 병상 신뢰`.

## 6. symptom escalation threshold /ˈsɪmptəm ˌɛskəˈleɪʃən ˈθrɛʃˌhoʊld/

## 7. preventive reminder fatigue /prɪˈvɛntɪv rɪˈmaɪndər fəˈtiːɡ/

**Core meaning (English):** declining attention to health reminders after receiving too many repeated prompts.

**Grammar & collocations:** `severe preventive reminder fatigue` — fatigue; `prevent preventive reminder fatigue` — prevent.

## 9. patient autonomy belief /ˈpeɪʃənt ɔːˈtɑnəmi bɪˈliːf/

## 10. waiting-list fairness /ˈweɪtɪŋ lɪst ˈfɛrnəs/

## 11. second-opinion motive /ˈsɛkənd əˈpɪnjən ˈmoʊtɪv/

## 20. patient safety norm /ˈpeɪʃənt ˈseɪfti nɔrm/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** a shared expectation that everyone should identify, report, and prevent avoidable harm in care.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** expectation shared mọi người identify, report và prevent harm avoidable trong care.
**Grammar & collocations:** `strong patient safety norm` — norm; `reinforce a patient safety norm` — reinforce.
**Examples:** `A patient safety norm made the nurse question a medication label that looked almost identical to another.` → Norm safety patient khiến nurse question label medicine gần giống label khác.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `환자 안전 규범`.

## Review in context

Patients interpret care through **diagnostic uncertainty perception** and **appointment effort cost**. **Treatment side-effect salience** can outweigh expected benefit, while a safe **medical default** reduces forgotten follow-up. **Provider bedside trust** shapes disclosure, and a **symptom escalation threshold** determines when help is sought. Repeated prompts can create **preventive reminder fatigue**, while **health literacy friction** makes instructions hard to use. **Patient autonomy belief** supports questions, and **waiting-list fairness** affects acceptance of delay. A **second-opinion motive** may protect against irreversible choices; **placebo expectation** can influence reported comfort. **Adherence reward framing** and **care coordination trust** make routines easier, while a **hospital reputation cue** shapes first impressions. **Medical bill shock** and low **treatment uncertainty tolerance** can stop care. Reducing **caregiver decision burden**, **health insurance option overload**, and strengthening a **patient safety norm** make the system more humane and reliable.

**Bản dịch tiếng Việt:**

Patient interpret care qua **diagnostic uncertainty perception** và **appointment effort cost**. **Treatment side-effect salience** có thể lấn expected benefit, còn **medical default** safe giảm follow-up forgotten. **Provider bedside trust** shape disclosure, và **symptom escalation threshold** quyết định khi nào seek help. Prompt lặp tạo **preventive reminder fatigue**, còn **health literacy friction** làm instruction khó use. **Patient autonomy belief** hỗ trợ question, và **waiting-list fairness** ảnh hưởng acceptance delay. **Second-opinion motive** bảo vệ khỏi choice irreversible; **placebo expectation** influence comfort report. **Adherence reward framing** và **care coordination trust** làm routine dễ hơn, còn **hospital reputation cue** shape impression đầu. **Medical bill shock** và **treatment uncertainty tolerance** thấp có thể stop care. Giảm **caregiver decision burden**, **health insurance option overload** và strengthen **patient safety norm** làm system humane và reliable.
