# Tense review — a working revision protocol

> Status: draft study guidance, to be checked during future lessons. This is a practice plan, not a claim of mastery.

## 1. Decide the reference time

Before you choose a verb form, ask: **What is my reference point: now, a past moment, or a future moment?**

- **I worked yesterday.** / 어제 일했어요. / Hôm qua tôi làm việc.
- **I was working when you called.** / 전화했을 때 일하고 있었어요. / Khi bạn gọi, tôi đang làm việc.
- **I had finished before you arrived.** / 당신이 오기 전에 끝냈어요. / Tôi đã xong trước khi bạn đến.
- **I've worked here for three years.** / 이곳에서 3년째 일하고 있어요. / Tôi làm ở đây được ba năm tính đến bây giờ.

The perfect form takes a **past participle (V3)** after `have`, `has` or `had`. In ordinary past questions, `did` is followed by the base verb: **Did you work?** — not *Did you worked?*

## 2. Review by observed error, not by chapter number

**Round A — Verb forms.** Say each question aloud before writing: *Did you go? Have you gone? Had you gone?* Check V1 vs V3, then rewrite missed words once from memory.

**Round B — Time contrast.** Contrast *I met him yesterday* with *I've met him before*. Use *I've been studying* to emphasize an activity continuing or recently completed, and *I've studied three chapters* when focusing on a result.

**Round C — Natural speech.** Answer three real questions: *What do you usually do after work? What have you been working on lately? What were you doing when someone called you last night?* Listen for missing articles and prepositions, not only tense.

## 3. Spaced review routine

- After class: write down the exact incorrect and corrected sentence.
- Next day: test yourself without the answer, and mark errors as **Đang luyện** only when you can explain the correction.
- After three more days: retest the same idea in a different context.
- One week later: tell a short story without a script; mark **Tạm vững** only if the structure was accurate in production.

These intervals are a suggested study practice, **not** automated scheduling. The dashboard tracks manual statuses and new attempt scores.

## 4. Three-language check

For one difficult sentence, explain in three steps: (1) English structure; (2) Korean meaning; (3) Vietnamese nuance. Avoid a word-for-word mapping between English Perfect, Korean `-았/었어요` and Vietnamese `đã`—their contexts do not always align.
