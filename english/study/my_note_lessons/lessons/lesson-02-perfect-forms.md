# Lesson 02 — Perfect forms and past deductions

**Level:** B1–B2  
**Goals:** connect two time points, distinguish perfect simple/continuous, and make deductions about the past.

## Present perfect simple

`have/has + past participle`

- experience without a finished time: `I have visited Korea three times.`
- present result: `She has lost her keys.`
- unfinished time period: `I have eaten three times this week.`

Use **since** for a starting point (`since 2020`) and **for** for a duration (`for five years`). Do not use present perfect with a finished time such as `yesterday`; use past simple instead.

## Present perfect continuous

`have/has been + V-ing`

Use it for an activity that started in the past and is still continuing, or for recent activity with visible evidence: `I have been learning English for five years.` / `You are tired because you have been running.`

## Past perfect continuous

`had been + V-ing` describes an ongoing activity before a past moment and emphasizes duration or cause: `She was tired because she had been studying for hours.`

## Perfect modals

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| regret/criticism | `should have + pp` | `I should have checked first.` |
| unreal past possibility | `could have + pp` | `She could have gone.` |
| certain past deduction | `must have + pp` | `They must have left.` |
| possible past deduction | `might have + pp` | `He might have been asleep.` |
| impossible past deduction | `can’t have + pp` | `They can’t have left by boat.` |
