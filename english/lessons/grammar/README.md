# English Grammar — A Natural American English Learning Path

This grammar library is meant to be read continuously, not scanned like a formula sheet. The previous band-based files were useful as coverage checklists, but they encouraged the learner to jump from one pattern to another before building a stable mental model. The canonical curriculum now follows conceptual dependency: first understand how an English sentence is organized, then how speakers identify things and quantities, then how English builds time and viewpoint, then modality and clause relationships, then how those structures behave in real conversation and inside larger clauses, and only after that move into counterfactuality, agency, compression, information structure, register, and advanced rhetorical choices.

The default variety is contemporary General American English. British English differences are connected directly to the relevant concept whenever they affect grammar, vocabulary, collocation, meaning, or real-world interpretation. The goal is to keep one stable American production standard while building enough British-English links that international books, media, exams, and coworkers do not require a second lookup process.

The explanations are layered inside ordinary prose. When a technical term is necessary, the lesson gives a precise grammar-reference-style definition and immediately turns it into plain language, often with the kind of concrete explanation that would also make sense to a child. These layers are deliberately mixed into the same explanation rather than split into separate “technical,” “dictionary,” or “easy” sections. The wording is self-contained and paraphrased so the reader does not need an outside grammar dictionary to understand the idea.

## Read in this order

Before the individual lessons, read [The Grammar Concept Map](00-concept-map.md). It gives one mental model for the entire curriculum and explains why sentence structure, reference, tense, modality, conversation grammar, embedded clauses, conditionals, agency, information flow, register, and advanced compression belong to the same system rather than to separate grammar lists.

Then start with [How an English Sentence Works](01-foundations/01-how-an-english-sentence-works.md). Continue directly to [Reference, Nouns, Articles, and Quantity](01-foundations/02-reference-nouns-articles-and-quantity.md), then [Time, Tense, and Aspect](01-foundations/03-time-tense-and-aspect.md), [Modality, Negation, and Questions](01-foundations/04-modality-negation-and-questions.md), [Verb Patterns and Connecting Ideas](01-foundations/05-verb-patterns-and-connecting-ideas.md), [Spoken American Conversation Grammar](01-foundations/06-spoken-american-conversation-grammar.md), and [Noun Clauses and Embedded Questions](01-foundations/07-noun-clauses-and-embedded-questions.md). Together these lessons establish sentence architecture, reference, tense and aspect, modality, questions, negation, complementation, passive voice, reported speech, relative clauses, conditions, the basic logic for joining ideas, the way complete clauses are contracted or echoed in conversation, and the way a proposition or question can become one grammatical component inside a larger sentence.

After the foundation sequence, continue with [Perfect Meaning and Narrative Time](02-core/01-perfect-meaning-and-narrative-time.md), [Conditionals, Counterfactuality, and Wish](02-core/02-conditionals-counterfactuality-and-wish.md), [Modal Perfects, Deduction, and Evaluation](02-core/03-modal-perfects-deduction-and-evaluation.md), [Voice, Reporting, and Clause Compression](02-core/04-voice-reporting-and-clause-compression.md), [Focus, Emphasis, and Information Flow](02-core/05-focus-emphasis-and-information-flow.md), and [Causatives, `get`, `have`, and Agency](02-core/06-causatives-get-have-and-agency.md). These lessons deepen the same systems rather than introducing a new “band”: temporal reference points, unreal alternatives, modal stance toward past events, passive and reporting systems, reduced clauses, clefts, inversion, focus, discourse flow, and the distinction between the participant who initiates an event, the participant who performs it, and the participant affected by it.

The final sequence is [Register, Subjunctive, and Formal Choices](03-advanced/01-register-subjunctive-and-formal-choices.md), [Hedging, Stance, and Evidential Grammar](03-advanced/02-hedging-stance-and-evidential-grammar.md), [Marked Word Order and Rhetorical Focus](03-advanced/03-marked-word-order-and-rhetorical-focus.md), [Ellipsis, Compression, and Nominalization](03-advanced/04-ellipsis-compression-and-nominalization.md), and [American–British Bridges and Native Choice](03-advanced/05-american-british-bridges-and-native-choice.md). These lessons are about control rather than complexity for its own sake: choosing the correct register, using the American mandative subjunctive naturally, calibrating certainty to evidence, recognizing rare or literary syntax without overusing it, compressing information without making prose opaque, and mapping important American and British differences into one connected grammar system.

The library is intentionally not divided into A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, or C2 files. Those labels can still be useful for assessment, but they are poor conceptual boundaries. A learner may understand an advanced conditional only after understanding tense as viewpoint, and may understand a C1 inversion only after understanding ordinary auxiliary movement. The files therefore follow prerequisites rather than exam bands.

No lesson treats “advanced” as “use the longest or rarest structure.” Native control means choosing the simplest form that expresses the intended meaning, evidence, focus, agency, and social relationship precisely. Marked inversion, nominalization, passive voice, causatives, formal linkers, and dense syntax are presented as tools with costs and purposes, not as upgrades over ordinary English.

The separate [Error Notebook — Units 01–13](error-notebook-unit-01-13.md) is a correction notebook rather than part of the canonical reading sequence. The six old `english-structures-*-revised.md` band files are removed from the canonical curriculum so the website exposes one grammar path rather than two competing structures.
