# C1 Vocabulary 40 — Behavioral climate finance and policy choice

This lesson follows how households, investors, and citizens interpret climate costs, incentives, fairness, and credibility when choosing action.

Flow: **carbon price perception → green tax salience → climate risk discounting → transition fairness belief → sustainable investment identity → offset credibility → greenwashing suspicion → energy subsidy framing → climate policy fatigue → adaptation co-benefit salience → carbon label trust → environmental norm enforcement → household footprint illusion → rebound climate effect → climate anxiety expectation → collective action efficacy → policy compliance climate → intergenerational fairness framing → loss aversion climate → net-zero credibility**.

## 1. carbon price perception /ˈkɑrbən praɪs pərˈsɛpʃən/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** a judgment about how high, fair, visible, and behaviorally important a carbon-related charge seems.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** judgment charge carbon high, fair, visible và important thế nào.
**Grammar & collocations:** `high carbon price perception` — perception; `change carbon price perception` — change.
**Examples:** `Carbon price perception changed when the rebate appeared beside the charge on the same bill.` → Perception price carbon đổi khi rebate appear cạnh charge cùng bill.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `탄소 가격 인식`.

## 2. green tax salience /ɡriːn tæks ˈseɪliəns/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** how noticeable and influential an environmentally motivated tax is in everyday choices.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** mức tax vì environment noticeable và influential trong choice daily.
**Grammar & collocations:** `low green tax salience` — salience; `increase green tax salience` — increase.
**Examples:** `Green tax salience rose when the receipt showed the charge next to the fuel purchase.` → Salience tax green tăng khi receipt show charge cạnh purchase fuel.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `녹색 세금 현저성`.

## 3. climate risk discounting /ˈklaɪmət rɪsk dɪˈskaʊntɪŋ/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** giving too little weight to future climate risks because they seem distant, uncertain, or politically inconvenient.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** weight quá ít cho climate risk future vì distant, uncertain hoặc inconvenient.
**Grammar & collocations:** `severe climate risk discounting` — discounting; `counter climate risk discounting` — counter.
**Examples:** `Climate risk discounting made the project look profitable after ignoring future flood damage.` → Discounting risk climate làm project trông profitable sau ignore flood damage future.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `기후 위험 할인`.

## 4. transition fairness belief /trænˈzɪʃən ˈfɛrnəs bɪˈliːf/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** a belief about whether the costs, jobs, benefits, and voice in an economic transition are shared fairly.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** belief cost, job, benefit và voice trong transition economy share fair.
**Grammar & collocations:** `strong transition fairness belief` — belief; `strengthen transition fairness beliefs` — strengthen.
**Examples:** `Transition fairness belief improved when affected workers received training before the plant closed.` → Belief fairness transition improve khi worker affected receive training trước plant close.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `전환 공정성 신념`.

## 5. sustainable investment identity /səˈsteɪnəbəl ɪnˈvɛstmənt aɪˈdɛntəti/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** a sense that investing in lower-impact activities expresses who a person or institution is.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** sense invest activity impact thấp express identity person hoặc institution.
**Grammar & collocations:** `strong sustainable investment identity` — identity; `build sustainable investment identity` — build.
**Examples:** `Sustainable investment identity made the fund publish its voting record rather than only its returns.` → Identity investment sustainable khiến fund publish voting record thay chỉ return.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `지속 가능한 투자 정체성`.

## 6. offset credibility /ˈɔfˌsɛt krɛdəˈbɪləti/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** confidence that a purchased environmental offset represents a real, additional, and lasting reduction.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** confidence offset represent reduction real, additional và lasting.
**Grammar & collocations:** `low offset credibility` — credibility; `protect offset credibility` — protect.

**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `상쇄 신뢰성`.

## 7. greenwashing suspicion /ˈɡriːnˌwɑʃɪŋ səˈspɪʃən/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** doubt that environmental claims exaggerate or disguise an organization's actual impact.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** doubt claim environment exaggerate hoặc disguise impact thật organization.
**Grammar & collocations:** `high greenwashing suspicion` — suspicion; `reduce greenwashing suspicion` — reduce.

## 8. energy subsidy framing /ˈɛnərdʒi ˈsʌbsədi ˈfreɪmɪŋ/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** the way an energy subsidy is presented as relief, investment, fairness, or a signal to change behavior.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** cách present subsidy energy như relief, investment, fairness hoặc signal change behavior.
**Grammar & collocations:** `equity-focused energy subsidy framing` — framing; `change energy subsidy framing` — change.

**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `에너지 보조금 프레이밍`.

## 9. climate policy fatigue /ˈklaɪmət ˈpɑləsi fəˈtiːɡ/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** declining attention or motivation after repeated climate announcements, consultations, and changing rules.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** attention hoặc motivation giảm sau announcement, consultation và rule climate lặp.
**Grammar & collocations:** `serious climate policy fatigue` — fatigue; `prevent climate policy fatigue` — prevent.
**Examples:** `Climate policy fatigue reduced participation even when the new proposal affected household bills.` → Fatigue policy climate giảm participation dù proposal mới ảnh hưởng bill household.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `기후 정책 피로`.

## 13. household footprint illusion /ˈhaʊsˌhoʊld ˈfʊtˌprɪnt ɪˈluːʒən/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** underestimating a household's total environmental impact by focusing on one visible behavior.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** underestimate impact environment total vì focus một behavior visible.

## 14. rebound climate effect /ˈriːbaʊnd ˈklaɪmət ɪˈfɛkt/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** increased consumption that offsets part of the environmental benefit of an efficiency improvement.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** consumption increase offset một phần benefit environment của efficiency improvement.
**Grammar & collocations:** `possible rebound climate effect` — effect; `measure the rebound climate effect` — measure.
**Examples:** `Rebound climate effect appeared when cheaper heating encouraged longer indoor hours.` → Effect rebound climate xuất hiện khi heating cheap encourage indoor hour dài.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `기후 반동 효과`.

## 18. intergenerational fairness framing /ˌɪntərˌdʒɛnəˈreɪʃənəl ˈfɛrnəs ˈfreɪmɪŋ/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** presenting climate decisions in terms of duties and benefits shared across present and future generations.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** present climate decision theo duty và benefit shared giữa generation hiện tại và future.
**Grammar & collocations:** `persuasive intergenerational fairness framing` — framing; `use intergenerational fairness framing` — use.
**Examples:** `Intergenerational fairness framing connected today's infrastructure spending with children's future options.` → Framing fairness intergenerational connect spending infrastructure hôm nay với option future của child.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `세대 간 공정성 프레이밍`.

## 19. loss aversion climate /lɔs əˈvɝʒən ˈklaɪmət/
**Part of speech:** noun
**Core meaning (English):** giving greater emotional weight to feared climate-related losses than to uncertain future gains from action.
**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** weight emotional cho loss climate feared lớn hơn gain future uncertain từ action.
**Grammar & collocations:** `strong loss aversion climate` — aversion; `address loss aversion climate` — address.
**Examples:** `Loss aversion climate made the farmer reject a profitable crop change because the first season felt risky.` → Aversion loss climate khiến farmer reject crop change profitable vì season đầu risky.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `기후 손실 회피`.

**Nghĩa cốt lõi & hình ảnh ghi nhớ:** confidence claim net-zero có plan, reduction, timeline và accountability measurable.
**Grammar & collocations:** `low net-zero credibility` — credibility; `restore net-zero credibility` — restore.
**Examples:** `Net-zero credibility rose when the company published interim targets instead of only a distant promise.` → Credibility net-zero tăng khi company publish target interim thay chỉ promise xa.
**Liên kết tiếng Hàn:** `넷제로 신뢰성`.

## Review in context

Citizens evaluate **carbon price perception** and **green tax salience** before deciding whether climate costs feel fair. **Climate risk discounting** can hide future harm, while a **transition fairness belief** determines who is asked to pay. **Sustainable investment identity** motivates action, but **offset credibility** and **greenwashing suspicion** determine trust. **Energy subsidy framing** can protect households, whereas **climate policy fatigue** reduces attention. Showing **adaptation co-benefit salience** makes action practical, and **carbon label trust** supports comparison. **Environmental norm enforcement** shapes daily behavior, but a **household footprint illusion** can hide large impacts. A **rebound climate effect** may reduce gains; **climate anxiety expectation** can paralyze without **collective action efficacy**. A fair **policy compliance climate**, **intergenerational fairness framing**, and attention to **loss aversion climate** support credible action. Ultimately, **net-zero credibility** depends on visible progress rather than distant language.

Show **adaptation co-benefit salience** làm action practical, và **carbon label trust** support compare. **Environmental norm enforcement** shape behavior daily, nhưng **household footprint illusion** che impact lớn. **Rebound climate effect** giảm gain; **climate anxiety expectation** paralyze nếu thiếu **collective action efficacy**. **Policy compliance climate** fair, **intergenerational fairness framing** và attention tới **loss aversion climate** support action credible. Cuối cùng, **net-zero credibility** phụ thuộc progress visible thay language distant.
